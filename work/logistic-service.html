Overview

Making complex freight operations easier to navigate

I was the UX/UI product designer on a transportation management platform that brings together freight loads, customer and carrier records, and admin workflows. I turned requirements into interface structures, prototypes and consistent interactions across connected modules.

The project is covered by an NDA, so the case shows selected design problems without naming the client or revealing confidential data. The examples focus on the desktop workspace; a responsive mobile interface for field operations was part of the project too.

The challenge

One set of data, many ways of working

A user may scan a list of loads, spot an exception, inspect a shipment, update a record or check the customer and carrier behind it. Each task needs a different level of detail. Three questions guided the design:

  1. 01

    What should users see when reviewing many records?

  2. 02

    Which information belongs in the view of a single load?

  3. 03

    How can the workspace adapt to different needs without becoming inconsistent?

Process

Structure first, details later

I started with what shapes the rest of the platform: navigation, system controls, information hierarchy and status visualisation.

  1. 01

    Structures

    Navigation, controls, hierarchy and status visualisation defined before screens.

  2. 02

    Validate

    User surveys and low-fidelity prototypes tested the key workflows.

  3. 03

    Test

    An interactive high-fidelity prototype went to user testing; the findings shaped the release.

01 — Loadboard

Dense on purpose, manageable by design

The Loadboard is the central module for reviewing and managing freight loads. People compare many records without opening each one, so the density is deliberate. Structure, filters and customisation make it manageable.

  1. 01

    Views users define

    Choose and order columns, pin key fields, set a default view. Fields are grouped and searchable.

  2. 02

    Hierarchy in dense rows

    Alignment, typography, labels and compact markers separate identifiers, statuses, places and dates; status and priority carry more weight.

  3. 03

    Selection and action

    Selecting rows opens an action context in the table. States, menus, editable cells and dialogs form one interaction model.

02 — Status and risk

Progress and urgency are different signals

Status labels show where a load is. Priority markers, flags and risk indicators show what needs attention.

Risk combines colour with labels, icons and context, so the meaning does not depend on colour alone. The same signals repeat across rows, details and related components.

03 — Load details

From comparison to investigation

The Loadboard is for scanning; the preview is for looking into one shipment, from a compact overview to operational detail. A route map gives a spatial view and the stop sequence a timeline of the same journey. An explicit edit action separates reviewing a record from changing it.

    Status and financial summary

  2. 02

    Route and tracking

  3. 03

    Shipment requirements

  4. 04

    Carrier information

  5. 05

  6. 06

04 — Customer profiles

A relationship that grows over time

Contacts, billing, preferences, credit limits and account ownership live in one profile. Sales teams start with a small set of data and complete it as the relationship develops, so an early start does not need every account detail.

05 — Carrier profiles

A clear hierarchy instead of one long form

Compliance, billing and relationship details are split into meaningful areas. That gives consistent access to carrier data across brokerage workflows and a reusable structure for carrier records.

06 — Beyond the Loadboard

The work around a load

Load management lists give a filtered view of open and active loads with status, equipment and rates. The tender area collects the requests that need action, and the bid view puts rate details next to the actions to book or bid. An adjustments screen brings the financial side of a load into the same system.

07 — User management

Access and responsibility, made readable

Users, roles and permissions get a dedicated area. Structured role definitions and activity information give context for administrative decisions. The work is about the clarity of these interactions; enforcing security belongs to the underlying system.

08 — Design system

One set of patterns for every module

Forms, tables, menus, dialogs, navigation and status information share patterns, with variants, variable-based styling and theme support. Familiar controls make the modules feel like one product, so design effort goes to the domain questions: which information matters, how to group it and what action follows.

Reflection

Density versus clarity

Showing less was not always the answer: in some workflows people need to compare many records at once. So the design relies on configurable views, consistent signals and a clear step from overview to detail.

The project shaped my approach to enterprise products: understand the operational context, set reusable interaction rules and let the structure of the workspace support different tasks.
